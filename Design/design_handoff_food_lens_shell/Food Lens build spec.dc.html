Build spec · draft 1 · Aug 2026

Food Lens shell rebuild

Replace the nine-card stack on /food and /compass with one scroll surface, and gate the vision loop on whether the user can actually see the camera.

Design intent, not final copy — every published number is unchanged. Companions: Food Lens redesign (turns 4a, 5a, 6a) and Food Lens prototype v2 scroll.

Sequencing: this lands after the core bets (P5+). It must therefore be a drop-in for a facts card that already carries every spec-24 block — each block moves as-is into a named slot. No block is rewritten by this work. Migration is per-block and reversible.

1 · Scope

In scope: page shell, scroll geometry, sticky strip, pinned voice bar, the visibility gate, and the capability props that let one component serve two routes.

Explicitly out of scope: the scoring engine, the FNDDS match logic, the safety gate and crisis pathway, the Tufts attribution, and the content of any spec-24 feature block. This work re-ranks and re-hosts; it does not re-compute.

2 · Shell geometry

One vertical scroll container. Four regions, top to bottom:

Region Height Behaviour
Viewfinder 336px Scrolls away normally. Not sticky.
Status strip 44px position: sticky; top: 0. Same height in both of its two modes.
Content auto The block slots, in fixed order (§4).
Voice bar 76px collapsed position: sticky; bottom: 0. Transcript expands upward, max 180px.

336px is a constraint, not a preference. It is the largest viewfinder that still puts the band chip, the verdict sentence and the score in the first 844px screen. At 440px the verdict landed ~500px down and the user arrived on a camera with no answer visible — which silently undoes the whole point of leading with a verdict. If the verdict block grows, this number shrinks.

The status strip carries loop status while the viewfinder is on screen, then the food's name and score once it is not — with a button back to the camera. Fixed height in both modes so nothing jumps at the switch, and the food name never appears twice on one screen.

3 · The visibility gate (rec 9 — new scope)

While less than half the viewfinder is on screen, send no frames. This is not in spec 24; it is the one recommendation here that adds a feature rather than re-ranking one.

3.1 Thresholds

Pause below 0.50 visible ratio; resume above 0.60, and hold the resume for one full interval before the first send. A single threshold flips repeatedly during a slow scroll; the hold stops a flick past the camera from costing a request.

3.2 Loop states

The prototype shipped two. Reality needs four, and the pill copy differs for each:

  • sending — armed, visible, a food identified or being sought. "Reading the camera · every 2.5s"
  • searching — armed and visible, nothing recognised yet. "Looking for food…"
  • paused-offscreen — visible ratio below threshold. "Camera paused — nothing sent"
  • unavailable — camera denied or absent. No pill at all — a permission error is not a loop state.

3.3 Integration with the existing disarm

  • F5 exposes disarmReason: "idle" | "provider" | null. Add "offscreen".
  • "offscreen" re-arms automatically when the ratio recovers. It must never route through rearm() and must never surface a "Scan again" chip. Getting this wrong makes scrolling back feel broken.
  • The idle disarm stays. A camera parked on screen while nobody looks still burns frames; AUTO_DISARM_MS is unaffected.
  • Pairs with the 60-second stash: scroll away and back inside the window and stashRef re-shows the last match instead of re-identifying. A scroll then costs zero calls, not one.
  • Crisis is a separate disarm, not a side effect. The crisis screen unmounts the camera, so a naive gate would read 0% and pause — correct by accident. Give it an explicit reason: "paused because you scrolled" and "stopped because we intercepted" must not share a code path.

3.4 Performance constraints (learned the hard way)

The first prototype build froze the tab. Scroll → setState → re-render content inside the scroller → layout → scroll is a live feedback loop. Required:

  • The visible ratio lives in a ref, never in component state. Any human-readable ratio readout is written imperatively.
  • Measurement is coalesced to one animation frame per scroll burst.
  • Only the on/off boolean triggers a render — and only when it actually flips.
  • No backdrop-filter and no drop-shadow inside the scroll container. Both were accomplices in the freeze.
  • Prefer IntersectionObserver with a threshold list over per-event geometry where the scroll root allows it.

3.5 Why this is a privacy feature first

"Frames go out only while the camera is on your screen" is a claim a patient can verify with their own thumb — which is more than any sentence currently in the disclosure copy can offer. Recommend it headlines the disclosure sheet rather than sitting in a card, and that someone checks whether it changes what the AI-data disclosure must assert. The cost saving is real but secondary.

On /compass it is also a spend control: an unauthenticated page with a live vision loop is the one surface where a stranger can bill you indefinitely. With the gate plus the idle disarm, an abandoned public tab costs zero. Ship the gate on the public mount first.

4 · Block slots

The content region renders a fixed sequence of optional slots. Each is an existing component, moved unmodified. A slot that has nothing to show renders nothing — not a heading with an empty body, not a greyed placeholder.

  1. verdict — band chip, sentence, number. Always present except carve-out and empty.
  2. plate — F11 item rows. Only at ≥1 item; the "plate average" label and its caveat appear only at ≥2 (see §6).
  3. chart — quadrant plot, your marker plus the single best alternative.
  4. whyScore — F9 domain breakdown. Opens from the chart marker; must scroll itself into view.
  5. weHeard — interpretation read-back and closer-category picker.
  6. flags — personalized warnings. /food only.
  7. totals — F10 running day totals. /food only.
  8. nutrients — per-serving panel and portion stepper.
  9. alternatives — better options list, with the Score/Calories toggle in its own header.
  10. actions — log, add to plate, secondary rows.
  11. attribution — Tufts, AI-assisted, not-medical-advice. Always last, always present.

5 · Two mounts, one component

Locked decision 5 freezes /compass as stateless, store-free and input-shape-frozen. The routes therefore do not merge; they share the shell and differ by a capability object.

Every difference is a prop, not a route check. No if (pathname) anywhere in the shell. This keeps decision 5 testable: the public mount can assert its own capability set, and e2e/compass.spec.ts:56 keeps passing because the text box is never rendered — not hidden.

6 · Rules the layout must enforce

These are correctness requirements, not styling. Each has a failure mode that misrepresents a number.

  • A plate of one is not an average. Below two items the headline shows the published score with its band. At two or more it switches to "Plate average · N items" and gains the caveat line. Averaging language over a single published number under-claims a real value; the reverse over-claims a computed one.
  • The plate average is never plotted. Chart markers are published scores only.
  • A carve-out has no number slot. The 50px figure and the chart are absent, not zeroed or greyed — a dimmed "—" reads as a bad score. The carve-out sentence takes the verdict's position and type size.
  • Servings never move the score. They change nutrition and day totals only. State this in the portion block, once.
  • Every interactive target ≥44px. Includes the recents chips, correction chips and the trust pill.
  • Quadrant names live outside the plot. A colour-keyed 2×2 legend beneath it, with the user's quadrant marked. The plot corners hold data.

8 · Strings

19 new English strings, 38 across both dictionaries. This number has been revised three times as more of the shipped dictionary was read — treat 19 as the current best estimate and have the implementer reconcile it against strings.ts before committing to a copy review. Six candidates were dropped on discovering they already ship (compassWhyScore, compassPublishedDriversNote, compassNotAssessable, compassNoPublishedScore, compassPointAtFood, and the carve-out sentences).

Drafts for all of them are in Food Lens prototype (v1), grouped by area, awaiting an editing pass. Two are safety-adjacent — "Voice paused for your safety" and the crisis lock note — and belong in safetyStrings under the safety approval path, not a copy review.

The three verdict sentences are the highest-risk strings in the set: they are the only place the product speaks a judgement in its own words rather than reporting a number. They should read the same way a good clinician talks — plain, unhedged, no diagnosis.

9 · Open decisions

  1. Disclosure consolidation. The design replaces three repeated guidance sentences with one pill and a detail sheet. Needs a compliance yes. If the answer is "inline," the shell still works — it just gets longer, and rec 6's benefit is lost.
  2. The public denied-camera dead end. /compass with no camera has no keyboard and no store — the only screen in the design where a user can arrive and do nothing. Sample scan is today's answer; confirm it is still the answer.
  3. Do the chart's alternative dots re-score on tap, or stay a read-only comparison?
  4. In-memory scan history on /compass. Not personalization and not the store: a session-only list, gone on reload, that would let the public surface hold a plate and compare scan to scan. Worth an owner call, separate from the merge question.
  5. Verdict sentence ownership. Drafted; needs an editor and an approver.

10 · Acceptance criteria

Every rule above maps to an assertion in an existing test file. The build is done when these pass, not when it looks right.

Hook tests — use-live-food-score.test.tsx:

  • Ratio 0.49 → disarmReason === "offscreen" and zero identify calls while paused.
  • Ratio 0.61 → no send until one full interval has elapsed; ratio 0.55 after a pause → still paused (hysteresis, not a single threshold).
  • rearm() never clears "offscreen"; ratio recovery is the only path back. The existing provider-rearm test (line 184) is the pattern.
  • Pause → recover inside 60s → stashRef re-shows the match with zero identifies.
  • Idle-disarm timing tests pass unmodified — the gate must not reset AUTO_DISARM_MS bookkeeping.

E2E — food-lens.spec.ts:

  • Scroll the viewfinder half off → pill reads "Camera paused — nothing sent" and the stubbed identify route records no new requests.
  • Scroll back → match re-appears, no "Scan again" chip anywhere in the DOM.
  • Strip switches loop-status ↔ name+score with no layout shift (fixed 44px), and the food name appears exactly once on any screenful.
  • One plate item → published score + band, the word "average" absent. Two items → "Plate average · 2 items" and the caveat line.
  • Carve-out → no 50px figure, no chart region, "Log it anyway" present.

E2E — compass.spec.ts:

  • Existing assertions pass untouched — line 56 (no text input rendered) is the capability-prop proof.
  • Gate active on the public mount; no camera button in the strip; camera-denied path lands on sample scan, not a dead end.

Performance gate: with the loop armed, a full-speed scroll of the content region triggers zero content re-renders (assert with a render-count probe, per §3.4). Review checklist: no backdrop-filter or drop-shadow inside the scroller.

11 · Telemetry

§14 names the risk this design accepts; these counters are how we find out if it was the wrong trade. Counts and durations only — never frames, and no food names on the unauthenticated mount.

  • gate_pause / gate_resume — pause duration and frames skipped. The cost-saving claim in §3.5 becomes a measured number.
  • stash_hit vs reidentify_on_return — is the 60s window long enough?
  • strip_camera_tap — the §14 watch metric. High rates mean people miss the always-on camera; that is the signal to revisit a sheet.
  • verdict_visible_ms — arrival to verdict-in-viewport. The 336px constraint (§2) exists to keep this near zero; regression here means the viewfinder grew.
  • voicebar_expand — whether anyone reads the transcript.

12 · Accessibility

The e2e suite finds everything by role — region "Food camera", role "log" for the transcript. Re-hosting a block must not strip its landmark; the tests are the tripwire.

  • Loop pill: aria-live="polite", announcing once per state flip — never per interval tick. In unavailable the node is removed, so nothing announces.
  • Status strip: a named landmark; its mode switch announces the new content politely. The camera button is the strip's only control — reachable, ≥44px, first in strip tab order.
  • whyScore: opening from the chart marker moves focus to the panel heading; closing returns it to the marker. "Must scroll itself into view" (§4) means focus and scroll arrive together.
  • Reduced motion: transcript expand and strip transitions become cuts under prefers-reduced-motion. The gate itself is motion-neutral.
  • Chart: quadrant meaning is carried by the named legend (§6), never colour alone; the marker keeps a text alternative naming its quadrant.

13 · Wide viewports

/compass is public, so desktop arrivals are certain, not hypothetical. The answer is a clamp, not a layout: the shell is one column, max-width: 480px, centered. The viewfinder keeps its 336px height with the camera crop letterboxed; sticky geometry, gate thresholds and the voice bar are identical. A two-column desktop layout is explicitly out of scope — the only requirement is that nothing breaks or stretches full-bleed at 1440px.

14 · The known cost of this design

Under the draggable sheet the camera was always present — half-covered, but live. In the scroll model, reading the detail means the camera is gone. For someone standing in a grocery aisle comparing two packages, that is a real loss, and the Camera button in the status strip is a mitigation rather than a fix.

The trade is still right: no gesture to learn, a whole class of layout bug deleted, and an honest signal for "the user isn't looking" that the sheet could never provide. But it is the first thing to watch in a real usability test, and the finding that would justify revisiting a sheet.
