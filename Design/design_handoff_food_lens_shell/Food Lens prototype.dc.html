Try these, in order

1 · Drag the dark bar

Drag up and down, then release. It snaps to peek / half / full. A tap with no movement cycles to the next height instead — that 8px threshold is what keeps drag and tap from fighting.

2 · Tap the transcript line

The two-line status expands into a scrollable log inside the handle. From peek it lifts the sheet to half first — see the note below for why it has to.

3 · Tap the mic

Listening ↔ paused. The waveform stills and the button hollows out. Note it never drags the sheet even though it sits on the handle.

4 · Scan next, then Add to plate

Cycle foods with "Scan next" and add two or three. The headline flips from a published score to a calorie-weighted plate average — relabelled, not just renumbered. Adjust quantities and watch it move; remove items to get back to a single food.

5 · Tap the marker on the chart

The filled dot opens the domain breakdown and pushes the sheet to full. Plate items appear as hollow dots — the plate average is never plotted, because it isn't a published score.

What I found building it

  • Drag and tap can share the handle, but only with a movement threshold. Without it every drag also fires the transcript.
  • The sheet needs two independent scroll surfaces — the transcript log and the content — or expanding the log steals the content's scroll position.
  • A growing handle can eat its own sheet. Opening the log at peek height pushed the handle past 258px, so the flexible content pane collapsed to zero and the verdict rendered below the phone. Two guards: opening from peek lifts the sheet to half, and the log's height is now derived from the sheet's, so it can never out-grow its container. Caught only by tapping it in the state the prototype opens in.
  • The mic has to stop propagation explicitly. A 54px button on a drag handle is otherwise a drag handle.
  • Plate mode changes the headline's meaning, not just its value, so the label and the sub-line both have to switch. That's the trap-2 discipline, enforced in layout.
  • A plate of one should not say "plate average" — it is still a published score, and averaging language there under-claims a real number. The switch happens at two items. I only noticed this by building it.
  • Tapping the marker has to scroll the panel into view. Opening it below the fold is the same dead end the old page had.
