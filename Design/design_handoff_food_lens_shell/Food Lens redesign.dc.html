The tool has a score problem, not a scoring problem.

Today /compass stacks nine equal-weight cards under a half-height viewfinder, and the same Food Compass number is drawn four times in four different ways. /food is a second, divergent implementation of the same viewfinder. Meanwhile voice — the thing the demo is actually about — is a status pill on the camera and a transcript log printed below the methodology footer.

The proposal keeps every published number, the Tufts attribution and the safety copy, and rebuilds the surface around one camera, one scroll, one verdict, and a voice bar pinned where your thumb already is. The quadrant chart stays prominent — and starts doing real work. (An earlier draft of this proposal used a draggable sheet; 4a records why building it changed my mind.)

The shell takes a capability object, not a route check

Every ✕ above is a prop, not an if (pathname). That keeps decision 5 testable: the /compass mount can assert its own capability set, and e2e/compass.spec.ts:56 keeps passing because the text box was never rendered — not hidden.

Rec 9 makes the public mount cheaper than the private one

An unauthenticated page with a live vision loop is the one surface where a stranger can spend your budget indefinitely. Gating on visibility means a /compass tab left open on a desk sends nothing — and combined with the idle disarm, an abandoned session costs zero. That is a stronger argument for shipping rec 9 on the public mount first.

The one question this leaves

A public mount with the pinned voice bar and no keyboard has no path at all when the camera is denied — no store, no favourites, nothing to fall back to. Today it offers a sample scan. Worth confirming that's still the answer, because it is the only screen in the design where a user can arrive and do literally nothing.

Ports, and what they taught

Mechanical: carve-out, no-match, Spanish

Same blocks in the same order, below a 336px viewfinder, above a pinned bar. All three benefit: their content is short, so it now fits the first screen entirely — the carve-out sentence and its two actions land above the fold, which the sheet's peek height could not guarantee.

Crisis has to kill the loop, not just the layout

In 3a I said crisis takes the whole screen. Rec 9 adds a harder requirement: the vision loop must stop too. The camera is unmounted, so a naive visibility gate would read 0% and pause — correct by accident. It should be an explicit disarm with its own reason, because "paused because you scrolled" and "stopped because we intercepted" are not the same state and must not share a code path. The pinned bar is replaced by the locked strip; the mic and keyboard are both dead until acknowledge.

The gate needs a fifth state: nothing to send

Empty and camera-denied both surfaced it. "Sending" and "paused because off-screen" don't cover a camera that is on screen, armed, and looking at a countertop — nor one that cannot open at all. The pill needs a third face ("Looking for food…") and denied needs no pill whatsoever. Two states in the prototype, four in reality.

Correcting the string count, again

Reading the dictionary properly for this port found that several strings I counted as new already ship: compassWhyScore, compassPublishedDriversNote, compassNotAssessable, compassNoPublishedScore, compassPointAtFood, and every carve-out sentence — including the exact alcohol line I drew. That removes 6 of the 22.

This port adds 3 back: the loop pill's three faces. So the honest figure is 19 new English strings, 38 across both dictionaries — down from my 22/44. Third revision of this number; the spec should own it, not a design doc.

4a

The shell changed — recommendations 1 and 2 are superseded

Building the sheet is what killed the sheet. The draggable peek / half / full model works, but it asks the user to learn a gesture, it produced a real bug where a growing handle collapsed its own content pane, and it gave the product no honest signal for "the camera isn't being looked at." The chosen shell is one scroll surface — and it brings a new recommendation that isn't in spec 24 at all.

Replaces rec 1

One camera, one scroll

Nine stacked cards still collapse to one surface — but it is an ordinary scroll, not a sheet with three snap heights. A 336px viewfinder at the top, a 44px sticky status strip, then the content. No gesture to teach; scrolling is a model people already have.

336px is not arbitrary. At 440 the verdict landed ~500px down an 844px screen, so you arrived on the camera and had to scroll to learn anything — quietly undoing rec 3. At 336 the band, the sentence and the number all sit in the first screen with the chart's top edge peeking.

Replaces rec 2

The voice bar is pinned, not dragged

It sticks to the bottom of the viewport at every scroll position, so the mic is in the thumb arc without a gesture to discover. The transcript expands upward inside the bar and the page keeps its place. Voice is still structural — it just stops being load-bearing for layout.

The sticky strip does two jobs in one 44px row: loop status while the camera is up, then the food's name and score once it has scrolled away, with a button back. Same height either way, so nothing jumps — and the food's name is never printed twice on one screen.

New · rec 9 · not in spec 24

Gate the vision loop on viewfinder visibility

When less than half the viewfinder is on screen, stop sending frames. Resume above 60% and hold one interval, so a slow scroll can't flip the loop on and off and a flick past the camera doesn't fire a request.

A draggable sheet over a fixed camera has no honest way to know the user stopped looking. Scroll position is exactly that signal, for free — which is the strongest argument for the scroll model, beyond the gesture.

Rec 9 — what it needs from the loop

  • A fourth disarm reason. F5 exposes disarmReason: "idle" | "provider" | null. Add "offscreen" — and unlike the other two it must re-arm automatically when the camera returns, never through rearm() and never showing a "Scan again" chip. Get that wrong and scrolling back feels broken.
  • It pairs with the 60-second stash. Scroll away and back inside a minute and stashRef should re-show the last match rather than re-identify — a scroll then costs zero calls, not one.
  • It does not replace the idle disarm. A camera parked on screen while nobody looks still burns frames; AUTO_DISARM_MS stays.
  • The ratio must never enter component state. A render per scroll event, where the render itself changes content inside the scroller, is a scroll → render → layout → scroll loop; it froze the tab in my first build. Ratio in a ref, readout written imperatively, measurement coalesced to one frame, only the on/off boolean re-renders. Keep backdrop-filter and drop-shadow out of the scroller too — both were accomplices.

And the part worth arguing for: this is a privacy feature before it is a cost one. "Frames go out only while the camera is on your screen" is a claim a patient can verify with their own thumb — more legible than anything currently in the disclosure copy, and a candidate to headline that sheet rather than sit in a card.

What survives from 3a, and what doesn't

  • Unchanged in substance: empty, carve-out, no-match, Spanish. The blocks are the same; they sit below a scrolling camera instead of inside a sheet.
  • Camera denied changes most. There is no sheet edge to hang a keyboard from, and a viewfinder that cannot render a feed should not hold 336px of a scroll surface — it collapses to a short explanatory block with the retry, and the typed controls ride the pinned bar.
  • Crisis is the one state that is still not a scroll surface — it takes the whole screen and the pinned bar is replaced by the locked strip. That claim survives intact and matters more now: the shell has to be able to stop being the shell.

What the scroll model costs

Under a sheet, the camera was always there — half-covered, but live. Now reading the detail means the camera is gone. For someone standing in a grocery aisle comparing two packages, that is a real loss, and the "Camera" button in the sticky strip is a mitigation, not a fix.

I still think the trade is right — the gate, the missing gesture and the deleted bug class outweigh it — but it is the thing to watch in the first real test, and the thing that would send me back to a sheet if patients feel it.

1 · The empty state is what recents are for

Today an unidentified food gives you a hint string and nothing to do. F14's recents and favorites were specced as a shortcut; their real job is giving the empty screen an action. No number, no chart, no log button — the sheet only ever shows blocks it can fill.

2 · A carve-out has no number slot at all

The 52px figure and the chart are absent, not zeroed or greyed — a dimmed "—" would read as a bad score. The carve-out sentence takes the verdict's position and type size, so the layout stays recognisable. "Log it anyway" stays, because the diary still wants the entry.

3 · Turn the dead end into a next move

"Try a simpler name" asks the user to guess what the table contains. Three tappable candidate phrasings answer the question instead — free, deterministic, and they teach the vocabulary of the published categories. New behaviour, not just new layout — worth flagging as scope.

4 · Honour what the string promises

The shipped copy says "you can still use the typed controls below" — so on a denied camera the keyboard becomes primary and the mic steps back, rather than a voice-first bar sitting above a promise nothing keeps. F5's retry lives on the overlay, /food only; /compass keeps its sample-scan fallback.

5 · The crisis state is not a variant of the sheet

Every other state keeps the camera and the voice bar. This one takes the whole screen: camera gone, voice bar replaced by a locked strip, three 56–60px call/text targets, the safety plan, and the acknowledge button. Copy is quoted verbatim from safetyStrings — no redesign touches these words. A camera-first shell must be able to stop being camera-first.

6 · Spanish survives, with one concession

The verdict headline wraps to three lines and the number holds its row — the reason it sits in a fixed-width column rather than flowing. The trust pill is the real win: the full es guidance sentence is 84 characters and could never be a one-line chip, but "Nutrición general ⓘ" can. That is rec 6 paying for itself in the harder language.

One source nit found while building it: compassCalorieDensity ships as "Densidad calorica" — unaccented, like its neighbours ("Calorias", "Azucares anadidos"). Quoted as-is here, but that region of the es dictionary wants an accent pass before F15 ships to Spanish-speaking patients.

Correcting my own count

In 2a I put the new-copy budget at ten strings. These six frames add twelve more, so the honest figure is twenty-two new English strings, forty-four across both dictionaries — and every one needs the same human authorship and approval path as the safety copy it sits beside.

  • From 1b / 2a (10): three band verdict sentences, the chart's plain-word caption, the plate-average sub-line — ×2 languages.
  • New in 3a (12): "Nothing in view yet" · "Point at a food — or pick one you've had before." · "Not scored" · the carve-out explanation · "Log it anyway" · "Why some foods aren't scored" · "No match" · "Say one of these instead" · "Mic ready — or type below." · "Or start from something you've logged" · "Voice paused for your safety" · the crisis lock note.
  • Not strings: the three candidate phrasings in frame 3 are published FNDDS category names read from the table, not authored copy — they need no translation, which is part of why that pattern is cheap.

Forty-four strings is a real cost and the exact list belongs in the build spec, not a design doc. Worth naming now, though, because it is the kind of number that turns up late and stalls a phase.

Recommendation-by-recommendation

Rec 8 · Withdrawn — I was wrong

Locked decision 5 freezes /compass: stateless, store-free, no personalization, no favorites, no text box (e2e/compass.spec.ts:56 asserts the input's absence). Merging the two tools is not available, and the General / For my plan switch I drew is wrong twice over — /food is always personalized, so it was a control for a choice nobody has.

Replacement: one component, two mounts. Same shell, viewfinder, voice bar, verdict, chart and legend. The /food mount adds the store, portion, plate, day totals, favorites, typed input and camera retry; the /compass mount adds none of them and takes ?lang=es. The switch becomes a non-interactive state chip: amber "For my plan" on /food, blue "General nutrition" on /compass. One design, two configurations — and no locked decision touched.

Rec 1 · Holds — and now it's load-bearing · revised by 4a

Spec 24 adds five blocks to the facts card (plate, Today so far, why-this-score, last-time-you-ate, recents/favorites). Nine stacked cards would become fourteen, and the argument for collapsing them into one surface only gets stronger. The surface is a scroll, not a sheet — I originally claimed peek/half/full was the only structure that could absorb it, which building it disproved. One ordinary scroll surface with a 336px viewfinder and a pinned voice bar does the same job with no gesture to learn.

Rec 2 · Holds, one addition · revised by 4a

The bar is pinned to the viewport now rather than riding a sheet edge, but everything below is unaffected. F4 puts a text input into live voice — so the voice bar gains a keyboard toggle beside the mic on the /food mount only. It must route through sendUserText (which gates first), never a raw response.create. The /compass voice bar shows no keyboard at all.

Rec 3 · Holds, needs a plate variant

"One verdict, one number" assumed one food. With a plate the headline number becomes the calorie-weighted plate average, labelled as such in the eyebrow, in the sub-line and in voice context — never "Food Compass score" (trap 2). Per-item published scores live in the rows at 40px chips. That's drawn at left.

Rec 4 · Holds, with one rule

The chart keeps two points and an arrow. But never plot the plate average — it is a display derivation, and a point among published scores would read as one. For a plate, plot the per-item dots. F13 also gives the marker a job: tapping it opens "Why this score?".

Rec 5 · Holds, but the chips differ per mount

I conflated two chip sets. interpretation (restaurant / topping / crust / size) exists only on the identify text path, so the live camera loop never returns it — those read-back chips are a /compass phenomenon. On /food the equivalent surface is F2 "Not this?" correction candidates plus the F3 spoken-size assumption. Same slot over the viewfinder, different content — and on /food a tap must pin the corrected match for 60 s so the vision loop can't overwrite it.

Rec 6 · Holds, with a distinction I missed

Spec 24 adds three new caveats — "read from the label photo, check the numbers" (F16), "the published number stands" (F13), "large ≈ 1.5 servings" (F3). These are per-food qualifiers, not disclosures: they stay inline, touching the number they qualify. Only the four global disclosures (guidance source, AI data use, transcript storage, ±14-point estimate note) collapse behind the pill. Otherwise "one quiet line" turns into hiding things that matter.

Rec 7 · Holds unchanged

Nothing in spec 24 touches the sort control. Segmented toggle in the "Better options" header, rendered only when options exist.

New in the design, from spec 24

  • Plate rows with per-item scores, steppers and a labelled plate average (F11).
  • Today so far bars — amber ≥80%, red ≥100%, hatched when data is incomplete, plus the cumulative escalation line (F10).
  • Why this score? — signed domain contributions and an explicit list of every unassessable domain (F13).
  • Last time you ate this, with the paired reading — screen only; voice gets the non-numeric form (F9, locked decision 7).
  • Recent & starred chips above the voice bar, one tap to re-score for free (F14).
  • "Scan again" in the camera chrome for the idle disarm, and camera retry on the denied overlay — /food only (F5).
  • Label-photo path surfaced as provenance in the header, not a badge in a corner (F16).

What this costs in strings

The invariant is no hardcoded English, both dictionaries. This turn's new copy: the verdict sentence, three bands × two languages = 6 strings, plus the chart's plain-word caption (2) and the plate-average sub-line (2). Ten so far — 3a adds twelve more once the edge states are counted, for 44 across both dictionaries. All human-authored, needing the same approval path as the safety copy. Everything else reuses existing keys.

F15 also means the design must survive Spanish at these type sizes — the verdict headline is the risk, since Spanish runs ~20% longer. It is set to wrap to three lines without pushing the number off the row.

Sequencing — decided

The shell lands after the core bets (P5+). That accepts a known cost — the facts card gets built twice, since every phase from P1 on adds a block to the card this design replaces — in exchange for not blocking the feature work.

It changes one thing in the design: the sheet has to be a drop-in replacement for a facts card that already carries every block, so each block becomes a named slot the shell accepts as-is rather than a rewrite. Migration is then per-block and reversible, and the phases in between don't have to hold still.

Still open

  • The compliance question from 1b stands, and is now narrower: four global disclosures behind one pill, per-food caveats staying inline. Acceptable?
  • Does "Log this plate · 3 items" read as one action to a patient, when it writes three entries sharing a mealId? I think yes, but it is worth one usability check before P5.
  • Sequencing above needs an owner call — it changes the phase plan, not the feature list.
  • Does "stateless" have to mean "no memory at all"? /compass avoids the store for a good reason — mounting it would hand a stranger Brent's care plan and readings, and the "no health profile used" claim is only honest because the page can't see them. But an in-memory list of what you scanned this session — gone on reload, never persisted, no patient data — would let the public surface hold a plate and compare scan to scan, which is the most useful thing it lacks. That isn't personalization and it doesn't touch useHealthState. Worth deciding separately from the merge question.

The restaurant / toppings / crust / size read-back is the most convincing thing in the demo, and it is nested three levels inside the result card, after success. Nothing before that tells the user those four details are what to say.

7

Methodology outranks the conversation

The transcript log — the primary interaction — is the last section on the page, printed under the "How scoring works" footer. In source order, the voice UI is literally an afterthought.

8

Chart type below the accessibility floor

Axis ticks and quadrant names are 10–11px in an app whose Privacy page ships a large-text mode. The chart the user is asked to read is the smallest type on screen.

9

Pantry search has the weight of a primary action

A full-width 56px bordered button sits directly under "Start", so the two loudest controls on /food are "talk to me" and a side quest.

10

One card carrying six jobs

Portion stepper, nutrient panel, disclosure chip, Compass score, collapsed alternatives, personalized flags and the log button are all one flat card with no internal hierarchy.

11

Two tools, one lens

/food and /compass share FoodViewfinder and diverge on everything else — two conversation panels, two result cards, two score presentations to maintain and two things to demo.

1b

Proposed — one camera, one sheet, three heights

Superseded by 4a. The draggable peek / half / full sheet drawn here was replaced by a single scroll surface with a pinned voice bar. Recommendations 3–8 still hold; 1 and 2 do not. Kept for the reasoning.

Same palette, same 8px radius, same published numbers. The page becomes a viewfinder with a draggable sheet, and the sheet's top edge is the voice bar — so the microphone always lands in the thumb arc, at every height. Nothing new is invented; the existing pieces are ranked.

What changes, and why

1 · One camera, one sheet

Nine stacked cards become a full-bleed viewfinder plus a sheet at three heights: peek (verdict), half (chart + options), full (read-back, detail, actions). One scroll surface, one place to look, and the demo becomes a single gesture instead of a scroll hunt.

2 · The sheet's top edge is the voice bar

Waveform, last two turns and a 56px mic ride the sheet edge at every height — always in the thumb arc, never scrolled away. Voice stops being a status pill and becomes the structure. Tap the transcript to open the full log.

3 · Say the answer once, in words

"A lot of calories for the nutrition you get" — one sentence, one 52px number, one band chip. The four competing ring dials collapse into the chart marker and the alternatives list. Band words stay Encourage / Moderate / Minimize in the data; the human sentence does the explaining.

4 · The chart earns its prominence

Kept big, and now it does the comparing: two points and one arrow — your food, and the single best published alternative — so "better" reads as a direction instead of a cluster. The other options stay in the list, where there is room for their numbers. The four quadrant names move out of the plot corners (the data lives in those corners) into a colour-keyed 2×2 legend beneath it, with your quadrant marked. Axis type goes 11px → 13px, legend 10px → 13px, your marker 32px → 44px.

5 · Read-back before success, not after

Restaurant / topping / crust / size become chips over the viewfinder the moment a food is identified — filled when heard, dashed when missing ("+ crust?"). They teach the vocabulary, show the system listening, and are tappable when voice mishears. This is the demo's money shot; put it on screen.

6 · One quiet trust line, details on tap

"General nutrition ⓘ" in the camera's top-right replaces three identical chips; the AI-data disclosure, transcript-storage note and ±14-point estimate caveat move behind it into one sheet. Every word is preserved — the count on screen drops from four blocks to one pill. Attribution stays permanently at the foot of the full sheet.

7 · Controls next to what they control

The sort fieldset becomes a Score / Calories segmented toggle in the "Better options" header, rendered only when options exist. Two taps saved, one always-empty control removed.

8 · One tool, two data modes

/food and /compass become the same screen. A General / For my plan switch in the full sheet turns on the personalized flags, the portion stepper and logging; /compass just opens locked to General with no patient record. One surface to design, ship and demo.

What I'd cut outright

  • The "Point / Talk / Compare" onboarding strip — the camera teaches it in two seconds.
  • Two of the three guidance chips per screen, and three of the four ring dials.
  • The standing "Find recipes in my pantry" button — same feature, secondary row in the full sheet.
  • The permanent sort fieldset and the "How scoring works" collapsed footer as a separate block.

Nothing above changes a published number, the safety gate, the crisis pathway or the Tufts attribution. It is all ranking and removal.

Open questions for you

  • Does compliance accept one disclosure pill with the full text one tap away, or must the sentences stay inline?
  • The verdict sentence is new human-authored copy per band — who owns and approves those three strings?
  • Should the chart's hollow alternative dots be tappable to re-score, or stay a read-only comparison?
